1. Experimental protocol

We evaluate the five descriptor-based k-nearest-neighbour classifiers (Section 2.a) and the majority-vote combination of their predicted classes (Section 2.c). All 18 classes of SharvitB2 are included: 12 images per class, for a total of 216 samples.

108one-vs-rest PR curves

Leave-one-out (LOO): for each of the 216 queries, the image is excluded from the reference set and its 12 nearest neighbours are drawn from the other 215 images. We use the Euclidean distance L2. The experiment makes 216 held-out predictions/scores per approach, and derives one curve for each of the 18 classes.

216 descriptors per approach→LOO: 215 references→12 nearest neighbours→One-vs-rest class scores→PR curves and AUC
Important distinction. Here K = 12 means twelve nearest neighbours, not twelve classes. Although each class has 12 images overall, for a query from that class there are only 11 other same-class examples in the reference set. The 12th neighbour must therefore come from some other class.

2. How precision–recall curves are constructed

Class scores

For each class C, we use a one-vs-rest assessment: its 12 members are positive examples and the other 204 images are negative examples. For each held-out query, the system produces a score for class C.

k-NN score: s(x, C) = [number of C-labelled images among 12 neighbours] / 12Vote score: v(x, C) = [number of descriptors predicting C] / 5

Each of ART, GFD, SA, Yang and Zernike7 produces its own k-NN score. For the majority-vote curve, the score is the fraction of the five descriptor-level decisions supporting the class. Note that the second score contains only six possible values (0, 0.2, …, 1), so its curve generally has fewer threshold steps than individual k-NN curves.

Threshold, precision and recall

Starting above the highest score, no image is selected: the curve starts at (recall 0, precision 1) by convention. As the threshold decreases, all images that have the same score are added simultaneously. Precision and recall are recalculated after each group is admitted.

Precision = TP / (TP + FP)     Recall = TP / (TP + FN)TP = true positives; FP = false positives; FN = false negatives.

When the threshold reaches zero, all 216 images are selected, so recall reaches 1 and precision reaches 12/216 = 0.055556. This is the positive-class prevalence (5.56%) and provides a reference level for interpretation. It is not the initial point of the curve.

Area under the curve (PR-AUC)

The supplied C++ code computes the area using the trapezoidal rule over the successive precision–recall points:

PR-AUC = Σi=1…m (Ri − Ri−1) × (Pi + Pi−1)/2Macro PR-AUC = (1/18) × Σc=1…18 PR-AUCc

This is trapezoidal PR-AUC, not ROC-AUC or Average Precision (AP). The curves use actual discrete score thresholds and are connected by straight segments in that order.

7. Interpretation of the experiment

GFD is strongest overall

GFD achieves macro PR-AUC 0.972694, with perfect AUC on 10 of the 18 classes. It never drops below 0.875558 (Class 8), indicating consistently high positive/negative ranking performance under these parameters.

Voting is a strong but not universal improvement

Majority voting ranks second, with macro PR-AUC 0.959262. Relative to GFD, voting is better on five classes, tied on five, and worse on eight. Its largest deficit is on Class 13 (0.831229 versus 0.974359), despite improving upon GFD on Class 18.

SA is the weakest descriptor

SA obtains macro PR-AUC 0.462869. Class 18 reaches only 0.074290 and Class 13 only 0.127630, although Class 6 reaches 0.959416. Thus, the weakness is strongly class-dependent.

Macro scores hide local failures

Zernike7 is third overall (0.919465), yet drops to 0.400000 on Class 15, where GFD reaches 1.000000. Yang (0.688178) also has difficult classes, notably C15 (0.342945), C9 (0.348078), and C4 (0.384720).

What can—and cannot—be concluded

These results assess the quality of class-score ranking, not a single classifier's recognition rate at one operating threshold. A high PR-AUC means the class's positives generally receive stronger scores than negatives across the tested thresholds. In contrast, the global recognition rate evaluates one final predicted label per query. Neither metric should be substituted for the other.

Important limitations. All 216 observations belong to one dataset; LOO repeats highly overlapping reference sets. Macro PR-AUC is descriptive, not a generalization guarantee or statistical significance test. Curves are discrete and may have tied score groups, especially voting (scores in fifths). The comparison uses one distance (L2) and one neighbour count (12), and does not establish those as optimal. The data do not identify which specific images were corrected by voting: this would require sample-level predictions.

8. Complete answer to the professor's question

Section 3.c — Precision–Recall curves (and AUC), K = 12

We evaluated the five k-NN classifiers based on ART, GFD, SA, Yang and Zernike7, plus the majority-vote combination of their predictions, on all 18 classes of SharvitB2 (216 images). The test protocol was leave-one-out: each image was scored using its twelve nearest neighbours among the remaining 215 images, with Euclidean distance (L2). For each class, we treated its 12 images as positives and all 204 other images as negatives, producing 18 one-vs-rest precision–recall curves for each method (108 curves in total).

For a single descriptor, a class's score is the fraction of its labels among the twelve neighbours. For voting, it is the fraction of the five descriptors whose k-NN decision supports that class. We varied the threshold, computed precision and recall at each level, and calculated PR-AUC using trapezoidal integration. We then averaged the 18 class AUC values to obtain each method's macro PR-AUC.

GFD provides the highest overall result (0.972694), followed by majority voting (0.959262), Zernike7 (0.919465), ART (0.852032), Yang (0.688178), and SA (0.462869). Voting improves markedly over weaker descriptors but does not surpass GFD's macro-AUC. The per-class curves expose important weaknesses that would be hidden by a single average: for example, SA has PR-AUC 0.074290 on Class 18, while Zernike7 has 0.400000 on Class 15 despite its good macro score.

We conclude that GFD offers the most reliable overall class-score discrimination among the tested approaches, while voting offers a strong combined solution with different class-specific strengths. These findings apply to the 18-class dataset with the specified LOO protocol, distance and neighbour count; further testing would be required to assess performance on independent data.
