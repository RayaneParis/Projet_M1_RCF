Interpretation

A · Does voting beat the best descriptor?

Voting ties the strongest individual result in nine settings. Only at L2, k=9 does it win: 70/72 versus 69/72 (+1.3889 pp). It adds some value, but the overall evidence is mainly that aggregation preserves the strongest accuracy rather than consistently improving it. Comparing against SA alone would exaggerate the benefit.

B · Does distance change voting?

L1 and L2 give identical vote scores at k=1, 3, 7 and 9. At k=5, L1 classifies one more image correctly (72 versus 71). Thus L1 is slightly better, not decisively better; the difference is only one result among 72 images.

C · Does increasing k help?

L1: 100 → 100 → 100 → 98.6111 → 97.2222. L2: 100 → 100 → 98.6111 → 98.6111 → 97.2222. Larger k does not improve the vote here. A wider neighborhood can include images from competing classes, weakening local discrimination; the aggregate rates alone cannot prove this is the exact cause.

D · Is voting more robust?

Voting loses only 2 correct images from its best to worst k for each distance. SA loses 8 images with L1 and 9 with L2; most other descriptors vary by 3. Voting is therefore comparatively stable, but ART and Zernike7 with L1 also vary by only 2. We cannot infer which image-level errors were fixed without individual predictions.
