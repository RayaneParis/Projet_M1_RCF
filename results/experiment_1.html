Why: ART, GFD and Zernike7 consistently score near the top, suggesting that their feature representations separate these six classes more effectively in this dataset. GFD with L1 retains 100% up to k=5; Zernike7 with L2 retains 100% up to k=3. ART achieves 100% at k=1 with either distance, but loses accuracy when more neighbors vote. Yang performs well (93.06%–97.22%) but does not reach 100%; SA is considerably weaker (70.83%–83.33%), suggesting more overlap between its representations of different classes.

These are conclusions about this 72-sample experiment, not proof that one descriptor is universally superior.

Comparison B — Influence of the distance

Compare the same descriptor at the same k across Table A (L1) and Table B (L2). Across all 25 matched comparisons, L1 wins 9, L2 wins 2, and 14 are ties. Every non-tie is just 1 correctly classified image out of 72 (1.39 percentage points).

Examples: ART at k=3 gives 98.61% with either distance (tie), while at k=7 L1 gives 98.61% versus 97.22% with L2. GFD at k=7 favors L2 (98.61% versus 97.22%); Zernike7 at k=3 also favors L2 (100% versus 98.61%).

Why: L1 sums absolute coordinate differences, whereas L2 gives greater influence to large coordinate differences through squared terms before taking the square root. That may change the closest neighbors and therefore the votes. L1 has a slight overall advantage here, but not consistently for every descriptor and k; the differences are small enough that it would be unjustified to claim a decisive distance advantage.

Comparison C — Influence of k

Read across each descriptor row. ART, GFD, SA and Zernike7 are best at k=1 (some remain tied for best at larger k). Yang is the exception: it rises from 95.83% at k=1 to 97.22% at k=3 with either distance, and L1 keeps 97.22% at k=5.

Concrete trends: ART–L2 falls from 100.00% at k=1 to 95.83% at k=9. SA–L2 falls from 83.33% to 70.83%. GFD–L1 stays at 100.00% for k=1, 3 and 5, then drops to 95.83% by k=9. Zernike7–L2 stays at 100.00% for k=1 and 3 before declining.

Why: With k=1, classification depends on the closest training example. Increasing k can make voting less sensitive to one atypical neighbor, which helps Yang at k=3. However, with these class representations, larger neighborhoods can include more examples from competing classes, changing the majority vote and lowering recognition. Therefore, increasing k does not automatically improve accuracy.

Interpretation: No single configuration is the unique winner. If choosing a setting based only on these results, GFD–L1 is a reasonable candidate because it remains perfect for three k values (1, 3 and 5), while Zernike7–L2 is perfect at k=1 and k=3. These patterns suggest more stability to k in the tested range, but they do not establish better out-of-sample generalization.

Important limitation: Leave-one-out tests each image against the other 71 images, but all observations come from the same small collection. Even 100% LOO accuracy is not a guarantee on new, independent images. Choosing a descriptor, distance and k after inspecting these results can also produce an optimistic estimate.
